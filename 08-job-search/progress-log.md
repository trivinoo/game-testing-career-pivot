# Job Search Progress Log

## October 2026 — The Pivot in Motion

After studying the fundamentals of Game QA and LQA (Linguistic Quality Assurance), I decided it was time to put things into practice and start actively pursuing opportunities in the field.

### Applications

I've applied for roughly two dozen positions related to **QA testing in games** — spanning roles like Game Tester, QA Analyst, LQA Tester (Portuguese), and similar titles across studios and outsourcing companies. The focus has been on finding an entry point where I can leverage both my software background and my growing knowledge of game-specific testing workflows.

### Market Research

Through the application process, I've been learning a lot more about the **current state of the game QA market**:

- Which companies and outsourcing agencies are actively hiring
- What tools and platforms are most commonly requested (JIRA, TestRail, DevTrack, etc.)
- Typical contract structures (full-time, contract, remote vs. on-site)
- How LQA roles differ from functional QA in terms of expectations and day-to-day work
- Salary ranges and career progression paths within game QA

### Networking

I've also been **connecting with professionals on LinkedIn** who are already working in Game QA and LQA. This has been incredibly valuable for:

- Getting firsthand insight into what the day-to-day work actually looks like
- Understanding which studios and outsourcing partners have better reputations
- Learning what hiring managers actually look for beyond the job listing
- Building a network that could lead to referrals down the line

### Next Steps

- Continue refining applications based on market feedback
- Follow up on active applications
- Keep building connections and engaging with the QA community
